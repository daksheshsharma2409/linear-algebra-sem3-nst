The above examples show that systems of linear equations can have no solutions, exactly one solution, or
inﬁnitely many solutions. We will show shortly that these are the only possibilities.
Theorem 1
—
Trichotomy for linear systems
Every system of linear equations has either
a) no solutions,
b) exactly one solution, or
c) inﬁnitely many solutions.
Note:
When a system of linear equations has at least one solution (i.e., in cases (b) and (c) of the
theorem), it is called consistent. If it has no solutions (i.e., in case (a) of the theorem), it is
called inconsistent.

Why do we use LU decomposition?
Imagine a huge mountain covered with dense forest. On this mountain, there are hundreds of
scenic spots. Each spot represents a di!erent right-hand side b in the system
Ax = b
The mountain itself never changes. Only the destination changes.
Without LU Decomposition (Gaussian Elimination Every Time)
Suppose someone asks you to reach Spot A. You begin at the base
and have no established path. So you cut through thick forest, climb
over rocks, avoid cli!s, and eventually reach Spot A. The next day,
someone asks you to reach Spot B. What do you do? You start all
over again. You cut through the forest again, clear bushes again,
and navigate around obstacles again. Every journey repeats almost
all of the hard work. This repeated e!ort is exactly what happens
when we perform Gaussian elimination every time we solve Ax = b.
The elimination process is like carving a temporary route through
the mountain from scratch.
With LU Decomposition
Now imagine that, instead of repeatedly hacking through the forest,
you spend one weekend constructing a sturdy staircase that climbs
the mountain. Building the staircase takes e!or but you do it only
once. Now someone says, “I want to reach Spot A.” You simply climb
the staircase until you’re approximately at the required elevation,
then take a short trail to the destination. The next person wants
Spot B. Again, you use the same staircase and leave it only near
the end. The di”cult part i.e. the climb, never has to be repeated.
Only the ﬁnal short walk changes.
Here
• Building the staircase =↗Computing the LU decomposition.
• Climbing to the appropriate base camp =↗Solving Ly = b (forward substitution).
• Taking the local trail to the ﬁnal viewpoint =↗Solving Ux = y (back substitution).
